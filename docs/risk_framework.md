# Risk Framework

## Purpose

The risk framework identifies the main ways a global investment thesis can fail. It is designed to keep research disciplined, especially when an opportunity appears cheap or neglected.

The central question is:

Is the expected outperformance sufficient to compensate for the risks that are not present, or are less severe, in the U.S. benchmark?

## Core Risk Categories

### Market Risk

Market risk includes broad declines in equities, bonds, commodities, or other risk assets. Global opportunities often appear attractive because they have already underperformed, but low valuation alone does not prevent further drawdowns.

Questions:

- How has the asset performed in prior global selloffs?
- What is the historical maximum drawdown?
- Is volatility structurally higher than the U.S. benchmark?
- Does the asset provide diversification, or does it sell off with U.S. equities during stress?

### Currency Risk

Currency risk can dominate local-market returns. An attractive local equity or bond market may still underperform in U.S. dollar terms if the currency depreciates.

Questions:

- Is the opportunity evaluated in local currency or U.S. dollars?
- Is the currency undervalued or overvalued on a real effective exchange-rate basis?
- Does the country have a current-account surplus or deficit?
- Are reserves adequate?
- Is carry sufficient to compensate for depreciation risk?
- Is hedging available and cost-effective?

### Political and Policy Risk

Political and policy risk includes elections, taxation, regulation, nationalization, capital controls, fiscal stress, corruption, and institutional instability.

Questions:

- Is the policy direction market-friendly, neutral, or hostile?
- Is the legal system predictable?
- Are foreign investors protected?
- Could capital controls or withholding taxes impair returns?
- Is there a credible central bank?
- Are reforms durable or dependent on one political figure?

### Geopolitical Risk

Geopolitical risk includes war, sanctions, trade restrictions, military escalation, maritime chokepoints, and diplomatic ruptures.

Questions:

- Is the asset directly exposed to conflict risk?
- Could sanctions prevent investment, liquidity, or repatriation?
- Is the country dependent on vulnerable trade routes?
- Is energy security a material concern?
- Could geopolitical risk create upside through defense spending, reshoring, or commodity scarcity?

### Liquidity Risk

Liquidity risk matters especially in frontier markets, small country ETFs, closed-end funds, and thinly traded ADRs.

Questions:

- What is average trading volume?
- Are bid-ask spreads acceptable?
- Can the position be exited under stress?
- Does the vehicle trade at a premium or discount to net asset value?
- Is the underlying market open when the U.S.-listed vehicle trades?

### Valuation Trap Risk

Cheap assets can remain cheap or become cheaper. A valuation discount may reflect structural impairment rather than mispricing.

Questions:

- Why does the valuation gap exist?
- Is the discount cyclical or structural?
- Are earnings, margins, or governance permanently impaired?
- Are minority shareholders treated fairly?
- What catalyst could close the gap?

### Concentration Risk

Country and sector ETFs may be highly concentrated in a few companies, sectors, banks, commodity producers, or state-linked firms.

Questions:

- What are the top holdings?
- What percentage is in the top ten holdings?
- Is sector exposure disguised as country exposure?
- Is the thesis really about one company, one commodity, or one policy regime?

### Data Risk

Data risk includes stale, revised, incomplete, or non-comparable information.

Questions:

- Is the data point-in-time or revised?
- Are valuation metrics trailing or forward?
- Are figures in local currency or U.S. dollars?
- Are ETF holdings current?
- Does the source methodology differ from other providers?

## Falsification Triggers

Each thesis should include explicit triggers for review or downgrade:

- Currency breaks below a key level or enters disorderly depreciation.
- Political event changes the investment regime.
- Earnings revisions turn negative.
- Inflation reaccelerates and forces tighter policy.
- Fiscal or external balances deteriorate.
- Commodity price assumptions fail.
- U.S. benchmark becomes more attractive on valuation or earnings momentum.
- Liquidity deteriorates materially.
- The core valuation gap closes without a corresponding improvement in fundamentals.

## Position Sizing Considerations

This project is not a personal financial planning tool, but research should still note implementation risks that affect sizing:

- Liquidity
- Volatility
- Correlation to existing U.S. exposure
- Currency exposure
- Geopolitical tail risk
- Instrument complexity
- Data confidence
- Time horizon

Higher-risk opportunities may still be attractive, but they require stronger expected return, better catalysts, or smaller sizing.

## Final Risk Standard

A thesis is not complete until the strongest bear case has been stated clearly. The goal is not to eliminate risk, but to determine whether the risk is understood, compensated, and monitorable.
