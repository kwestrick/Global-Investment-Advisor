# Investment Framework

## Objective

The Global Investment Advisor framework is designed to identify global market opportunities that may outperform U.S.-centric portfolios on a forward-looking, risk-adjusted basis. The core question is not whether an asset is interesting in isolation, but whether it offers a superior setup relative to U.S. alternatives.

The framework should be used for countries, regions, sectors, currencies, commodities, sovereign bonds, ETFs, ADRs, and globally listed equities. It can also be adapted for portfolio-level allocation decisions.

## Default Benchmarking Approach

Every thesis should define a benchmark before reaching a conclusion.

Common benchmarks:

- U.S. broad equity: S&P 500 or total U.S. equity market
- U.S. growth equity: Nasdaq 100
- U.S. small caps: Russell 2000
- U.S. fixed income: intermediate Treasuries or aggregate bonds
- U.S. cash: Treasury bills or money market yield
- U.S.-heavy balanced portfolio: 60/40 U.S. equity and bond allocation

The selected benchmark should match the opportunity. A Japanese bank equity thesis, for example, should be compared against U.S. financials and broad U.S. equities. A Brazilian local-currency bond thesis should be compared against U.S. Treasuries, U.S. credit, and U.S. dollar cash.

## Opportunity Categories

### Country and Region Opportunities

Country and regional theses should evaluate:

- Valuation relative to history and U.S. benchmarks
- Earnings momentum
- Fiscal and current-account position
- Central bank policy direction
- Inflation trend
- Currency valuation and reserve position
- Political stability and policy credibility
- Commodity exposure
- Foreign investor positioning
- Liquidity and investable vehicles

### Sector and Theme Opportunities

Sector and theme theses should evaluate:

- Structural demand drivers
- Supply constraints
- Policy support or policy risk
- Margin cycle
- Capital expenditure cycle
- Relative valuation
- Competitive position versus U.S. peers
- Global revenue exposure
- Sensitivity to interest rates, energy prices, and currency movements

### Currency Opportunities

Currency theses should evaluate:

- Real effective exchange rate valuation
- Interest-rate differentials
- Inflation differentials
- Terms of trade
- Current account and fiscal balance
- Reserve adequacy
- Political and sanctions risk
- Carry, volatility, and drawdown profile

### Commodity Opportunities

Commodity theses should evaluate:

- Supply-demand balance
- Inventory levels
- Capital expenditure cycle
- Geopolitical chokepoints
- Substitution risk
- Policy constraints
- Producer equity leverage
- Futures curve structure
- Currency and inflation sensitivity

### Sovereign Debt Opportunities

Sovereign debt theses should evaluate:

- Real yield
- Inflation trajectory
- Fiscal sustainability
- External debt profile
- Currency risk
- Central bank credibility
- Political stability
- Liquidity and market access
- Spread relative to comparable countries

## Scoring Model

A simple five-category model can be used for initial ranking.

| Category | Weight | Description |
|---|---:|---|
| Valuation | 25% | Cheapness versus history, peers, and U.S. alternatives |
| Macro Tailwind | 25% | Growth, inflation, policy, currency, and capital-flow support |
| Catalyst Strength | 20% | Clear events or trends that could close the valuation gap |
| Risk Control | 15% | Political, liquidity, balance-sheet, currency, and drawdown risks |
| Implementation Quality | 15% | Availability of liquid, transparent, cost-effective vehicles |

Suggested score scale:

- 5: Strong positive
- 4: Positive
- 3: Neutral or mixed
- 2: Negative
- 1: Strong negative

Weighted score:

```r
score <- valuation * 0.25 +
  macro_tailwind * 0.25 +
  catalyst_strength * 0.20 +
  risk_control * 0.15 +
  implementation_quality * 0.15
```

Interpretation:

| Weighted Score | Interpretation |
|---:|---|
| 4.25 to 5.00 | High-conviction opportunity |
| 3.50 to 4.24 | Attractive but requires monitoring |
| 2.75 to 3.49 | Watchlist or tactical only |
| 2.00 to 2.74 | Weak thesis |
| Below 2.00 | Avoid unless conditions change |

## Required Thesis Sections

Each investment memo should include:

- Executive summary
- Asset or vehicle
- Benchmark comparison
- Time horizon
- Why this may outperform the U.S.
- Current valuation
- Macro backdrop
- Currency considerations
- Geopolitical considerations
- Catalysts
- What could go wrong
- Falsification triggers
- Scenario analysis
- Implementation notes
- Conviction rating

## Falsification Discipline

Every thesis should specify what would prove it wrong. Examples:

- Earnings revisions turn negative for two consecutive quarters.
- Currency depreciation overwhelms local-market gains.
- Political risk becomes structural rather than cyclical.
- Valuation discount reflects permanent impairment rather than temporary neglect.
- U.S. assets continue to offer stronger earnings growth without a valuation penalty.
- Liquidity deteriorates enough to make implementation impractical.

## Output Standard

The final conclusion should be direct but conditional. Avoid language that implies certainty. Use phrasing such as:

- “The setup appears attractive relative to U.S. alternatives if...”
- “The thesis depends primarily on...”
- “The main reason to avoid this opportunity is...”
- “The opportunity should be downgraded if...”

Every investment research output should end with:

This is research and analysis only, not personalized financial advice. Consult a qualified financial advisor before making investment decisions.
