# Data Sources

## Purpose

This document tracks the data sources used in the Global Investment Advisor project. The goal is to maintain a transparent research process, preserve reproducibility, and identify weaknesses in the evidence base before making investment conclusions.

Data should be categorized by type, update frequency, licensing status, and reliability. When possible, raw data should be saved separately from processed data.

## Data Storage Convention

Recommended folders:

```text
data/
├── raw/
├── processed/
└── external/
```

Use the following convention:

- `data/raw/`: downloaded or manually collected data that should not be edited
- `data/processed/`: cleaned datasets generated by R scripts
- `data/external/`: reference files, classifications, mappings, or manually maintained lookup tables

Recommended file naming:

```text
source_dataset_frequency_YYYYMMDD.ext
```

Examples:

```text
worldbank_gdp_annual_20260921.csv
fred_dxy_daily_20260921.csv
etf_holdings_country_monthly_20260921.csv
```

## Market Data

Potential sources:

| Data Type | Possible Source | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Equity prices | Yahoo Finance through `tidyquant` or `quantmod` | Useful for ETFs, ADRs, indexes, and listed securities |
| ETF prices | Yahoo Finance through `tidyquant` | Useful for country, sector, and regional proxies |
| Index levels | Yahoo Finance, Stooq, official index providers | Check methodology and currency denomination |
| Bond yields | FRED, central banks, finance terminals | Confirm whether nominal or real yields |
| Currency rates | FRED, Yahoo Finance, central banks | Track base currency carefully |
| Commodities | FRED, Yahoo Finance, EIA, IEA, exchange data | Futures structure may require specialized data |

## Macro Data

Potential sources:

| Data Type | Possible Source | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| GDP growth | World Bank, IMF, OECD, national statistical agencies | Annual and quarterly series may differ |
| Inflation | World Bank, IMF, OECD, national statistical agencies | Prefer CPI and core CPI where available |
| Current account | IMF, World Bank, national central banks | Important for currency vulnerability |
| Fiscal balance | IMF, World Bank, national finance ministries | Compare debt and deficit together |
| Policy rates | Central banks, BIS, FRED | Watch effective rates versus target rates |
| Money supply | Central banks, FRED | Useful for liquidity analysis |
| Demographics | World Bank, UN, OECD | Slow-moving but structurally important |

## Geopolitical and Policy Data

Potential sources:

| Data Type | Possible Source | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Political risk | World Bank governance indicators, Economist Intelligence Unit, Freedom House, proprietary risk sources | Definitions vary widely |
| Conflict data | ACLED, UCDP, IISS, SIPRI | Useful for regional risk analysis |
| Sanctions | OFAC, EU sanctions map, UN sanctions lists | Must be checked before investability assumptions |
| Defense spending | SIPRI, NATO, national budgets | Relevant for defense and industrial themes |
| Energy security | IEA, EIA, national energy agencies | Important for Europe, Asia, and commodity exporters |

## Valuation Data

Potential sources:

| Data Type | Possible Source | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Index P/E and P/B | MSCI factsheets, iShares, Vanguard, State Street, country ETF providers | ETF factsheets are practical but may lag |
| Earnings growth | ETF factsheets, index providers, analyst datasets | Confirm forward versus trailing |
| Dividend yield | ETF providers, index providers | Watch withholding taxes and payout cyclicality |
| Sector composition | ETF providers, index providers | Important for country comparisons |
| Holdings | ETF provider websites | Holdings can lag and may change |

## Data Quality Checklist

Before using a dataset in a memo, confirm:

- Source name
- Download date
- Frequency
- Units
- Currency
- Nominal or real terms
- Seasonally adjusted or not
- Index methodology, if applicable
- Revisions policy
- Missing values
- Outliers
- Whether the data is point-in-time or revised

## Known Pitfalls

- Market data can be adjusted for splits and dividends, which may affect comparisons to historical price references.
- ETF holdings and factsheets often lag current market composition.
- Valuation data can differ across providers because of methodology differences.
- Macroeconomic data is often revised after initial publication.
- Currency returns can dominate local-market returns.
- Local-market indexes may not be directly investable.
- Capital controls, sanctions, taxes, and withholding rules can make theoretical opportunities difficult to implement.
