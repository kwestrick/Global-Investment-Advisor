## Executive Summary

The original investment plan had a sound strategic core: assets should reflect the household’s spending currency, operational capacity, global opportunity set, and need for durable wealth preservation. The Model Council agreed that building some COP-denominated assets can be reasonable because part of future spending, property obligations, and household life are Colombia-linked. The council also agreed that the original plan should not be executed as written.

This revised plan reframes the strategy around four mutually exclusive buckets:

1. **USD operating and contingency reserve**
2. **COP spending reserve**
3. **Defensive long-term assets**
4. **Global growth assets**

This structure fixes the main defects identified by the council:

- The prior plan mixed financial assets, total net worth, and income-producing assets.
- The prior plan overstated the COP carry cushion by comparing CDT yield against zero-yielding dollars instead of yield-bearing USD alternatives.
- The prior plan treated Colombian FICs as an easy TES-access route, despite likely PFIC risk for a U.S. person.
- The prior plan double-counted the USD reserve by using it both as a source of monthly contributions and as deployable investment capital.
- The prior plan treated thematic equity ETFs as distinct asset classes even though they are equity risk.
- The prior plan included product and rule errors that could lead to incorrect execution.

The revised conclusion is:

> Build COP exposure only after tax, residency, and reporting gates are cleared; size that exposure by expected COP spending rather than by an arbitrary 80/20 asset ratio; preserve a hard USD liquidity reserve; and rebuild the global ETF portfolio as a simple core portfolio with clearly capped active tilts.

This is a planning document, not a trade ticket. It should be reviewed by a qualified cross-border tax professional and a licensed financial advisor before implementation.

## Governing Policy

### Objective hierarchy

When objectives conflict, use this order:

1. **Maintain operational security.** The household must always retain sufficient immediately accessible USD liquidity for spending, medical, travel, property, and administrative needs.
2. **Avoid tax and reporting traps.** Do not enter foreign investment structures that create disproportionate reporting, PFIC, residency, withholding, or estate complexity.
3. **Match known COP liabilities.** Build COP assets only to the extent they improve the ability to fund Colombian spending, property costs, and household obligations.
4. **Preserve the estate floor.** Maintain a durable margin above the $500,000 estate objective.
5. **Grow long-term real wealth.** Use diversified global public-market assets to compound over a 20- to 30-year horizon.
6. **Seek non-U.S. outperformance only when evidence supports it.** Do not force a bullish conclusion on non-U.S. assets.

### Hard constraints

- **USD liquidity floor:** Maintain at least $100,000 in immediately accessible USD accounts.
- **Early-warning liquidity trigger:** If immediately accessible USD liquidity falls below $150,000, stop new investment contributions and rebuild liquidity.
- **No leverage, options, or margin.**
- **No individual positions** in NVIDIA, Microsoft, Alphabet, Meta, Amazon, or similar concentrated AI mega-cap trades.
- **No foreign pooled funds** without explicit U.S. PFIC review.
- **No investment that requires high-frequency trading, daily monitoring, or complex execution across two countries.**
- **No execution before tax, reporting, custody, and beneficiary/access issues are understood.**

### Investment horizon

The long-term investment horizon remains 20 to 30+ years. The operational horizon is shorter because disability, two-country residence, account access, tax residency, and estate execution may create constraints that a standard long-horizon portfolio would ignore.

## Pre-Trade Gates

No new COP instruments, foreign funds, TES bonds, or major ETF reallocations should be implemented until these gates are cleared.

### Tax and reporting gate

Obtain written guidance from a qualified U.S.–Colombia cross-border tax professional covering:

- U.S. treatment of Colombian CDTs.
- U.S. treatment of directly held TES bonds.
- Whether any Colombian FIC, fiducia, or local pooled investment vehicle is a PFIC for U.S. tax purposes.
- Whether Form 8621 would be required for any proposed Colombian fund exposure.
- FBAR reporting requirements for Colombian bank and brokerage accounts.
- Form 8938 thresholds and filing requirements for specified foreign financial assets.
- Foreign tax credit treatment for Colombian withholding taxes.
- §988 ordinary income/loss treatment for foreign-currency debt instruments.
- Colombian tax residency day-count rules.
- Colombian withholding rates by instrument and residency status.
- Colombian wealth-tax exposure under current and proposed rules.

Useful source checks from the Model Council review:

- Fogafín states that deposit insurance applies up to COP 50 million per depositor per registered institution and includes CDTs ([Fogafín](https://www.fogafin.gov.co/preguntas-frecuentes)).
- The IRS states that U.S. persons with aggregate foreign financial accounts above $10,000 may need to file FBAR/FinCEN 114 ([IRS FBAR guidance](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/report-of-foreign-bank-and-financial-accounts-fbar)).
- The IRS provides separate Form 8938 rules for specified foreign financial assets ([IRS Form 8938 guidance](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/corporations/do-i-need-to-file-form-8938-statement-of-specified-foreign-financial-assets)).
- The IRS Form 8621 instructions govern reporting for Passive Foreign Investment Companies ([IRS Form 8621 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i8621)).

### Residency and estate gate

Before expanding Colombian financial exposure, document:

- Days physically present in Colombia over a rolling 365-day period.
- Whether Colombian tax residency may be triggered.
- Whether Colombian worldwide-income taxation could apply.
- How the Colombian home passes at death.
- Whether a U.S. will, Colombian will, trust, beneficiary designations, or power of attorney is needed.
- Whether non-citizen spouse rules or QDOT planning are relevant.
- Who can access U.S. and Colombian accounts during incapacity.
- Whether two-factor authentication, bank apps, and brokerage accounts are operable from both countries.

### Product verification gate

Before any trade, verify:

- ETF ticker, sponsor, fund name, expense ratio, holdings, and benchmark.
- Whether the fund is U.S.-listed or foreign-domiciled.
- Whether the vehicle creates PFIC or other foreign-fund issues.
- Whether a proposed Colombian institution is Fogafín-registered.
- Current CDT rate, term, early-withdrawal limitations, and insured amount.
- Current TES yield, maturity, duration, liquidity, custody route, and tax treatment.

Do not allocate percentages until these two amounts are fixed:

### Bond allocation policy

The council disagreed on the correct bond weight, so the revised plan does not hard-code 25%. Instead:

- Treat Social Security and VA Disability as a stabilizing income stream in the household risk budget.
- Keep the $100,000 USD floor outside the strategic allocation.
- Use bonds or TIPS to reduce drawdown and operational risk, not because the portfolio requires income for current spending.
- Consider a TIPS ladder if real-yield protection is more valuable than nominal bond ballast.
- Decide the final bond range after modeling expected drawdown, liquidity needs, and inflation exposure.

## COP Liability-Matching Plan

### Revised objective

The COP strategy should be framed as:

> Build a limited COP-denominated reserve capable of funding a defined period of Colombian expenses and property obligations, while avoiding foreign-fund tax traps and maintaining enough USD liquidity to remain financially secure.

This replaces the earlier statement that the portfolio must move from 97.4% USD to 80/20 USD/COP.

### Size by spending, not percentage

Estimate Colombian spending under three cases:

| Case | Monthly COP-Linked Spending | Target Coverage | Implied COP Reserve |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Low Colombia presence | TBD | 12 months | TBD |
| Base Colombia presence | TBD | 18–24 months | TBD |
| High Colombia presence | TBD | 24–36 months | TBD |

The existing Colombian home should be counted as a COP-linked asset for total net worth, but not as an income-producing asset. It may also create COP expenses, including property tax, utilities, maintenance, insurance, and administration.

### Preferred implementation hierarchy

1. **Direct insured CDTs** at Fogafín-registered institutions, staying within the insured cap per institution.
2. **Direct TES bonds** only after tax, custody, duration, liquidity, and withholding treatment are reviewed.
3. **Avoid Colombian FICs/fiducias** unless a cross-border tax professional confirms that PFIC and reporting consequences are acceptable.

### CDT ladder design

The original two-bank design should be replaced by an insured-cap-aware ladder:

| Rule | Rationale |
|---|---|
| Keep each institution at or below the Fogafín insured limit | Reduces idiosyncratic bank failure risk |
| Use multiple registered institutions if balances exceed one or two caps | Avoids uninsured concentration |
| Select institutions by insured status, rate, operational access, and simplicity | Do not pay excessive yield penalty for brand familiarity |
| Ladder maturities monthly or quarterly | Avoids one-date liquidity lockup |
| Track as-of date for every rate | Colombian rates can change materially |

### COP risk rules

Use explicit USD/COP quote logic:

- Higher USD/COP means the peso weakened.
- Lower USD/COP means the peso strengthened.

Revised triggers:

| Trigger | Action |
|---|---|
| USD/COP falls below a pre-set strength threshold, such as 3,000 | Slow or pause new COP purchases; COP hedge may be expensive |
| USD/COP rises above a pre-set weakness threshold, such as 3,600 | Consider accelerating COP purchases if tax gates are cleared |
| USD/COP rises above severe-stress threshold, such as 4,500 | Reassess whether COP depreciation has overwhelmed carry benefit |
| Colombia withholding or U.S. tax treatment materially worse than expected | Pause COP program and revise after-tax breakeven |
| Colombian tax residency risk increases | Pause until day-count and worldwide-tax exposure are reviewed |
| CDTs cannot be kept insured within acceptable operational complexity | Reduce target or accept a documented exception |

### Stage 0 — Freeze execution until gates are cleared

Do not execute the old Phase 1 or Phase 2 instructions literally. The following items must be resolved first:

1. Cross-border tax memo.
2. Residency/day-count review.
3. Colombian account reporting checklist.
4. Estate/access review.
5. Updated macro dashboard.
6. Sources-and-uses schedule.
7. ETF product verification.

### Stage 1 — Rebuild the plan in R

Update the R project to calculate:

- Total net worth by currency.
- Liquid financial assets by currency.
- Income-producing assets by currency.
- USD liquidity floor and early-warning buffer.
- COP spending reserve target by months of expenses.
- Strategic USD investable capital after reserves.
- Look-through equity exposures by country, sector, and currency.

### Stage 2 — Decide the COP reserve size

Choose a policy target such as:

- 12 months of projected Colombian expenses for conservative exposure.
- 18–24 months for base-case dual-country living.
- 24–36 months only if tax, liquidity, and currency risks are acceptable.

The target should be stated in COP and translated into USD only for reporting. The success metric should be spending coverage, not USD-relative outperformance.

### Stage 3 — Implement COP assets slowly

If tax gates are cleared:

1. Transfer a small test amount from USD to COP.
2. Verify exchange spread, fees, settlement timing, and bank access.
3. Buy the first insured CDT.
4. Confirm statement, maturity date, rate, withholding, and redemption rules.
5. Repeat only if operational process is smooth.
6. Maintain a schedule of maturities and insured limits.

### Stage 4 — Rebuild USD portfolio

After reserves and COP target are defined:

1. Calculate the actual strategic investable base.
2. Set core allocations.
3. Add only those tilts that survive look-through analysis.
4. Avoid redundant India/Japan exposure unless intentionally sized.
5. Use contribution-based rebalancing before taxable sales.

## Risk Register and Falsification Triggers

### Key risks

| Risk | Severity | Monitoring Indicator | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| COP depreciates enough to overwhelm carry | High | USD/COP, after-tax yield spread versus USD alternatives | Pause new COP purchases; reassess target |
| COP strengthens sharply before target is reached | Medium | USD/COP falls below strength threshold | Slow purchases; avoid buying expensive COP |
| Colombian tax residency triggered unintentionally | Very High | Rolling 365-day Colombia day count | Freeze foreign expansion; get tax advice |
| PFIC exposure from Colombian fund | Very High | Any FIC/fiducia/local fund purchase | Do not buy without written review |
| Fogafín limits exceeded | Medium | CDT balance by institution | Add institutions or reduce exposure |
| USD liquidity falls below early-warning level | High | Immediately accessible USD cash | Stop contributions |
| Thematic equity concentration | Medium | Combined active-tilt weight and top holdings | Cap or trim tilts |
| India premium fails to translate into shareholder return | Medium | Earnings revisions, valuation, INR, ROE, flows | Reduce India tilt |
| Estate/access plan incomplete | Very High | Will, POA, beneficiaries, account access | Prioritize legal work |
| Large unmodeled expense, including aircraft or health costs | High | Budget variance, one-time expenses | Rebuild reserve assumptions |

### Falsification triggers

The plan should be reviewed or changed if:

- The COP reserve target cannot be justified by spending coverage.
- After-tax COP yield no longer exceeds USD alternatives by a sufficient margin for the risk taken.
- Tax professionals advise that Colombian instruments create unacceptable reporting or tax complexity.
- USD liquidity would fall below $150,000 under the next 12-month plan.
- ETF implementation cannot be reconciled with look-through exposure limits.
- The Colombia home materially changes in value, cost, legal status, or intended use.
- Guaranteed income assumptions change.
- Disability, care, mobility, or access needs materially change.
