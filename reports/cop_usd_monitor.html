What Is Driving the Historically Strong Peso? (Sep 2026 Analysis)

Primary Driver: Dollar Weakness (DXY –8.9% from Jan 2025 Peak)

The DXY (US Dollar Index) peaked at ~110 in January 2025 and has fallen to ~100 as of September 2026 — a broad weakening of the dollar against all major currencies. EM currencies including COP tend to appreciate mechanically when the dollar weakens, since commodity trade and capital flows are USD-denominated. This accounts for the majority of COP's appreciation from its 4,480 peak.

Secondary Driver: Risk-On Environment (VIX 14.8, down 71.7% from April 2025 peak)

VIX spiked to 52.3 in April 2025 (likely a global shock event) and has collapsed to 14.8 — an extremely calm equity market. Low volatility encourages capital flows into higher-yielding EM assets. With Colombia's TES bonds yielding 11–12% nominal vs. U.S. Treasuries at ~4.5%, the carry trade attracts USD inflows that strengthen the peso.

Tertiary Driver: Elevated Brent Crude (+11.4% from Jan 2022)

Brent at ~105 USD/bbl provides fiscal support for Colombia. Ecopetrol (state-controlled, 88% government-owned) generates significant USD export revenues at current prices. While the 90-day rolling correlation between Brent and COP is near zero (0.004), suggesting oil is not the near-term driver, sustained high prices support Colombia's current account and sovereign creditworthiness.

⚠ Key Risk: Strength Is Globally Driven, Not Colombia-Specific

The peso's appreciation reflects global tailwinds (weak dollar, risk-on, carry trade) more than Colombia-specific improvement. The Petro government's fiscal posture (expanding social spending, oil transition rhetoric, budget deficits) represents a structural headwind that global tailwinds are currently overwhelming. If the dollar reverses (e.g., Fed turns hawkish, U.S. inflation re-accelerates) or risk sentiment deteriorates, COP could give back gains quickly.

⚠ Historical Context: Peso Strength Is Near Unprecedented (2.6th Percentile)

At 3,193 COP/USD, the rate is stronger than 97.4% of all observations over the past 5 years. The 5-year median is ~4,022 and the peak was 5,106 (2022). A reversion even to the 3-year median (~3,949) would represent a ~24% peso depreciation from current levels — a meaningful USD-equivalent loss on any COP-denominated assets not matched to COP obligations.

Implication for CDT Strategy

The analytical findings from the Model Council review remain intact: CDTs are a liability hedge (funding COP spending needs), not a yield play. At current rates, converting USD to COP is particularly unattractive on a pure return basis — you are acquiring COP at historically expensive levels. Fund the minimum necessary reserve (~$19,600 across BBVA + Bancolombia) on a scheduled basis, regardless of rate, to cover the 18-month spending target. Do not add opportunistically beyond that target until the signal turns Favorable (≥ 65th percentile).
