## 2. Questions for the Tax Professional

Please provide written guidance — not verbal — on each of the following before any COP instrument is purchased.

---

### 2.1 Colombian CDTs: U.S. Tax Treatment

**Background:** A CDT (Certificado de Depósito a Término) is a time deposit at a Colombian bank, analogous to a U.S. CD. Interest is paid in COP at a fixed nominal rate. The principal and interest are repaid in COP at maturity.

**Questions:**

1. **§988 classification:** Are Colombian CDTs "foreign currency contracts" or "section 988 transactions" under the Internal Revenue Code? If so, how are FX gains and losses on the principal treated — as ordinary income/loss or capital gain/loss?

2. **Interest income:** How is the COP-denominated interest income reported on a U.S. federal return? What is the applicable exchange rate for conversion (spot at payment date, average annual, or other)?

3. **FX gain/loss timing:** If a CDT matures at a different COP/USD rate than when it was purchased, when is the gain or loss recognized — at maturity, at rollover, or annually?

4. **Interaction with §911:** Not applicable (we are not claiming the foreign earned income exclusion), but please confirm.

5. **Net Investment Income Tax (NIIT):** Does CDT interest income subject the taxpayer to the 3.8% NIIT under §1411? (Note: VA Disability income is excluded from gross income; Social Security is partially includable.)

---

### 2.2 Colombian Withholding Tax Rate

**Background:** Colombia imposes a withholding tax (retención en la fuente) on CDT interest paid to account holders. The commonly cited rate for Colombian residents is 7%. The applicable rate for non-residents may differ.

**Questions:**

1. **Applicable rate:** Given that I am a U.S. citizen pursuing dual Colombian citizenship but currently not a formal Colombian tax resident, what withholding rate will Colombian banks apply to CDT interest — 7%, a higher non-resident rate, or another rate?

2. **Treaty implications:** The U.S. and Colombia do not have a bilateral income tax treaty. Confirm whether any other treaty or agreement affects the applicable withholding rate.

3. **Foreign Tax Credit (FTC):** Is the Colombian withholding on CDT interest creditable against U.S. federal income tax under §901/§904? Are there any limitation issues (e.g., passive basket, sourcing)?

4. **Net tax cost estimate:** Given my income profile (Social Security + VA Disability only, no W-2), what is the approximate effective U.S. federal tax rate on CDT interest after applying the foreign tax credit?

---

### 2.3 Colombian Tax Residency

**Background:** Colombia defines tax residency primarily based on day-count (183 days in a calendar year or 365 days over a 24-month period, among other criteria). My presence in Colombia is approximately 1–3 months per year.

**Questions:**

1. **Residency threshold:** Given my current Colombia presence pattern (1–3 months/year), do I currently qualify as a Colombian tax resident? If not, at what point would I trigger residency?

2. **Spouse citizenship impact:** Does my wife Anna's Colombian citizenship affect my Colombian tax residency status?

3. **Dual citizenship impact:** Once I obtain dual Colombian citizenship, does that automatically trigger Colombian tax residency, or is day-count still the controlling test?

4. **Income disclosure in Colombia:** If I become a Colombian tax resident, am I required to declare my U.S. Social Security and VA Disability income to Colombia's DIAN? Is VA Disability income treated differently than Social Security?

5. **Worldwide income taxation by Colombia:** If I become a Colombian tax resident, does Colombia tax my worldwide income including U.S.-sourced income? What is the applicable Colombian income tax rate for a dual citizen in this income bracket?

6. **Day-count record keeping:** What records should I maintain to document days in Colombia vs. the U.S. to support a non-residency position?

---

### 2.4 PFIC Status of Colombian Investment Vehicles

**Background:** Under U.S. tax law (§1297), a Passive Foreign Investment Company is a foreign corporation where either (a) 75%+ of gross income is passive, or (b) 50%+ of assets produce passive income. PFICs are subject to highly punitive U.S. tax treatment.

**Questions:**

1. **FICs (Fondos de Inversión Colectiva):** Do Colombian FICs qualify as PFICs for U.S. tax purposes? (Preliminary assumption: yes — please confirm or correct.)

2. **Fiducias:** Do Colombian fiducias qualify as PFICs? Or are they treated as foreign grantor trusts or other structures under U.S. law?

3. **Direct CDTs:** Do CDTs held directly at a Colombian bank (not through any pooled fund) constitute a PFIC? (Preliminary assumption: no — please confirm.)

4. **Direct TES bonds:** Do Colombian government bonds (TES) held directly in a brokerage account at Valores Bancolombia constitute a PFIC? (Preliminary assumption: no — please confirm.)

5. **PFIC elections:** If any planned vehicle is a PFIC, what elections are available (QEF, mark-to-market), and are any of them practically feasible for Colombian instruments?

6. **Safe avoidance strategy:** Confirm that the proposed strategy — direct CDTs + direct TES bonds only, no FICs, no fiducias — is the correct approach to avoid PFIC exposure.

---

3. **Form 8938 threshold:** What are the Form 8938 filing thresholds applicable to me given my filing status and residency (U.S. resident filing single or jointly)?

4. **Colombian property reporting:** Is the Colombia residential property (~1.2B COP) reportable on Form 8938 or any other U.S. tax form? (Note: it is real property, not a financial account.)

5. **Prior-year compliance:** I have held Colombian bank accounts for multiple years. If I was required to file FBAR in prior years and did not, what is the recommended remediation path? (Please reference the IRS Streamlined Domestic Offshore Procedures if applicable.)
